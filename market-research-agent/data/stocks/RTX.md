# RTX (RTX)

## Overview
RTX Corp is a large aerospace and defense company formed from Raytheon Technologies/United Technologies. It operates across Pratt & Whitney aircraft engines, Collins Aerospace avionics/interiors/systems, and Raytheon defense systems including missiles, sensors, air defense, and command-and-control.

## Bull thesis
- Defense demand is structurally supported by NATO rearmament, missile/air-defense replenishment, and geopolitical tension.
- Commercial aerospace recovery supports Collins and Pratt aftermarket revenue, where installed-base economics can be attractive.
- RTX has diversified exposure: commercial aero, defense electronics, missiles, and engines reduce reliance on any one platform.
- If Pratt engine inspection/remediation costs peak and fade, margins and free cash flow could inflect higher.

## Bear thesis
- Pratt & Whitney engine quality/remediation liabilities can pressure cash flow, margins, and investor confidence.
- Defense programs are large, fixed-price, politically exposed, and vulnerable to schedule/cost overruns.
- A valuation rerating may already discount part of the defense spending tailwind after strong sector performance.
- Commercial aero cyclicality and supply-chain constraints can limit near-term execution.

## Risks
- Engine recalls/technical issues; fixed-price defense contract losses; budget delays; export restrictions; supplier bottlenecks; pension/interest-rate effects; geopolitical or program concentration risk.

## Catalysts
- Evidence that Pratt remediation costs are contained; expanding missile/air-defense awards; margin recovery at Collins/Pratt; dividend/buyback growth; improved free cash flow guidance; major NATO/US contract wins.

## Long-term outlook
RTX is a high-quality, diversified aerospace/defense compounder if execution improves. Defense demand and commercial aftermarket should remain durable, but engine liabilities keep the risk profile above the cleanest peers.

## Conviction rating
Medium conviction: attractive end-market exposure and scale, offset by execution and engine-risk overhang.
