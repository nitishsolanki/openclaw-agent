# CRWV — CoreWeave, Inc.

**As of:** 2026-08-27  
**Sector / industry:** Information Technology / Cloud infrastructure and data-center services  
**Conviction:** Medium — exceptional demand and backlog, but very high leverage, concentration, and execution risk

## Snapshot

CoreWeave operates a purpose-built cloud platform for AI training and inference, combining NVIDIA GPU infrastructure, networking, storage, orchestration software, and managed services. Its differentiation is speed and specialization for large AI workloads; its economic model is closer to a highly leveraged data-center operator than to an asset-light software company.

CRWV was about **$87.35** on August 27, 2026, with a market capitalization of approximately **$48.1 billion** and negative trailing earnings. Against the midpoint of management's reported 2026 revenue outlook, the equity trades at roughly **3.8x forward sales**. That is not an obviously excessive sales multiple for a company growing this quickly, but the debt and capital intensity make sales-based valuation incomplete.

## Latest operating results

Q2 2026 revenue was **$2.575 billion, up 112% year over year**. Adjusted EBITDA was **$1.510 billion**, or a 59% margin, versus $753 million and 62% a year earlier. Adjusted operating income was $128 million, but GAAP operating loss was $49 million and GAAP net loss was **$626 million**; net interest expense alone was **$640 million**.

Revenue backlog was approximately **$104 billion** at June 30, excluding more than $25 billion of net new commitments added in early Q3. Active power reached **1.5 GW**, up nearly 500 MW during Q2. Backlog is valuable visibility, but it remains conditional on CoreWeave delivering capacity and customers continuing to perform under long-term contracts.

Management raised 2026 guidance to approximately **$12.4–$13.2 billion of revenue**, **$960 million–$1.15 billion of adjusted operating income**, and an exit revenue run-rate of about **$18.5–$19.5 billion**. The company also expects active-power expansion to continue, which implies another large financing and construction cycle.

Cash flow shows the central tension. First-half operating cash flow was **$3.7 billion**, while investing cash use was **$14.9 billion**, primarily for GPUs and data-center infrastructure. At June 30, cash and equivalents were $5.5 billion; total recourse debt was $31.4 billion and non-recourse debt was $3.7 billion. The filing lists **$35.6 billion of future principal payments**, including $4.4 billion in the remainder of 2026 and $6.2 billion in 2027. Total liquidity, including available borrowing capacity, was $15.6 billion.

## Bull thesis

- AI compute demand remains supply-constrained, allowing CoreWeave to place capacity under long-term contracts before or as it is deployed.
- The $104B backlog, Meta's approximately $21B agreement through 2032, OpenAI's approximately $6.5B commitment through 2031, and new relationships with Jane Street, Anthropic, and enterprises provide strong multi-year demand visibility.
- Scale can improve utilization, pricing, and operating leverage. Q2's 59% adjusted EBITDA margin and positive adjusted operating income are evidence that the model can work before GAAP profitability fully catches up.
- NVIDIA's strategic investment, early access to next-generation platforms, and CoreWeave's software/platform layer may help it remain a preferred AI-cloud partner rather than a commodity GPU renter.
- Inference, agentic AI, sovereign/defense workloads, and international expansion could broaden the customer base beyond a few large training customers.

## Bear thesis

- Leverage is the defining risk. Q2 interest expense was $640M, while GAAP operating income was negative; a slowdown in utilization or pricing could make refinancing and debt service much harder.
- Capital intensity is extreme: first-half investing cash use was nearly four times operating cash flow. Growth requires repeated access to debt and equity markets, creating dilution and refinancing risk.
- Customer concentration is high. The top three customers represented approximately 36%, 26%, and 10% of Q2 revenue, and the 10-Q says concentration is likely to continue. A delayed deployment, contract dispute, internal buildout, or spending cut by a major customer could have an outsized effect.
- GPU obsolescence and depreciation are real risks. New NVIDIA generations can make older fleets less competitive, while CoreWeave must maintain utilization and resale/renewal economics across a rapidly changing hardware cycle.
- Backlog is not equivalent to booked revenue or cash. It depends on power, construction, equipment delivery, commissioning, service availability, and customer performance; delays can push revenue out while financing costs continue.
- CoreWeave depends heavily on NVIDIA hardware and on third-party data-center/power partners. Supply, power, permitting, construction, cooling, and networking bottlenecks can limit execution.

## Catalysts

1. Q3 revenue and active-power delivery tracking toward the raised outlook.
2. Conversion of the early-Q3 commitments into deployed, billable capacity without another large margin or financing surprise.
3. Evidence that inference and enterprise workloads are diversifying revenue away from the largest customers.
4. Falling net leverage or improving free cash flow after the current buildout peak.
5. Continued access to lower-cost secured and unsecured financing, supported by long-term customer contracts.

## What would change the view

**Upgrade:** sustained revenue growth above 80%, on-time power/capacity deployment, adjusted operating income translating into genuine post-capex cash generation, and declining net leverage.  
**Downgrade:** missed buildout milestones, backlog cancellations or weak conversion, customer concentration worsening, higher financing costs, GPU utilization/pricing pressure, or capex remaining far above operating cash flow for multiple quarters.

## Bottom line

CRWV is one of the strongest pure-play ways to express a continued AI infrastructure buildout, and Q2 materially strengthened the demand and margin story. But the equity is effectively a leveraged bet on three things happening together: AI demand stays strong, CoreWeave deploys power and GPUs on schedule, and financing remains available on tolerable terms. I would classify it as **Medium conviction / buy only in a sized, high-risk position**, not as a core holding. The next proof point is not another backlog headline; it is backlog conversion into operating income and cash after interest and capex.
