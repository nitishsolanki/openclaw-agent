# General Dynamics (GD)

## Overview
General Dynamics is a diversified defense and aerospace company with businesses in Marine Systems, Combat Systems, Technologies, and Aerospace. It builds nuclear submarines and surface ships, armored vehicles, IT/mission systems, and Gulfstream business jets.

## Bull thesis
- Marine Systems has unusually durable demand from US Navy submarine and shipbuilding priorities, with long backlogs and high strategic importance.
- Combat Systems benefits from armored vehicle modernization and allied defense spending.
- Gulfstream provides commercial aerospace upside and a differentiated high-end business jet franchise.
- GD's diversified portfolio and historically disciplined management support steady cash generation.

## Bear thesis
- Shipbuilding is labor- and supply-chain-intensive; delays or cost overruns can pressure margins.
- Gulfstream is cyclical and sensitive to corporate spending, rates, and wealth effects.
- Growth may be slower than defense peers with more missile/space exposure.
- Defense budget delays can shift revenue timing and working-capital needs.

## Risks
- Navy procurement changes; fixed-price contract execution; skilled labor shortages; supplier constraints; Gulfstream certification/delivery delays; IT contract recompetes; political scrutiny of defense outlays.

## Catalysts
- Submarine and shipbuilding funding increases; margin recovery in Marine Systems; Gulfstream delivery ramp; new vehicle awards; stronger free cash flow conversion; dividend/buyback expansion.

## Long-term outlook
GD offers a balanced defense/aerospace profile with particularly valuable submarine exposure. The long-term case is steady compounding rather than rapid growth, with execution in shipyards and Gulfstream deliveries the key swing factors.

## Conviction rating
Medium conviction: durable franchises and backlog visibility, offset by shipbuilding execution risk and aerospace cyclicality.
