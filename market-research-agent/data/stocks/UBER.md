# UBER — Uber Technologies, Inc.

**As of:** 2026-08-27  
**Sector / industry:** Industrials / Transportation and mobility technology  
**Conviction:** High — strong network effects, durable cash generation, and improving margins; regulatory and autonomous-vehicle risks remain

## Snapshot

Uber is a global marketplace connecting consumers with drivers, couriers, restaurants, merchants, and freight carriers. Mobility remains the core platform, while Delivery and Freight increase user frequency and improve cross-selling. Uber's most important asset is its global demand, supply, payments, pricing, mapping, and logistics network—not ownership of vehicles or restaurants.

UBER was about **$76.95** on August 27, 2026, with a market capitalization of approximately **$157.8 billion** and a trailing P/E near **16.8x**. The valuation is reasonable for a company growing bookings in the low twenties, generating more than $10B of trailing free cash flow, and still expanding margins.

## Latest operating results

Q2 2026 Gross Bookings rose **24% reported and 22% in constant currency to $58.0 billion**. Trips increased 18% to 3.9 billion, and monthly active platform consumers rose 16% to 208 million. Revenue was $14.2 billion, up 12% reported and 11% constant currency; business-model changes reduced reported revenue growth by 8 percentage points.

Adjusted EBITDA increased 33% to **$2.8 billion**, with the margin on Gross Bookings improving to 4.9% from 4.5%. Non-GAAP EPS rose 35% to $0.81. Q2 operating cash flow was $2.9B and free cash flow was **$2.8B**; trailing-twelve-month free cash flow exceeded $10B. Reported GAAP net income was $2.4B, but it included a $1.6B pre-tax benefit from equity-investment revaluations, so underlying earnings should be judged using operating income, non-GAAP earnings, and cash flow as well.

Delivery Gross Bookings grew 26% and Freight 25%, both faster than Mobility's 22%. Q3 guidance calls for **$58.25–$60.25B Gross Bookings**, 18%–22% constant-currency growth, non-GAAP EPS of $0.84–$0.88, and adjusted EBITDA of $2.86–$2.96B.

## Bull thesis

- Uber's two-sided network is difficult to replicate: scale improves ETAs, pricing, driver utilization, merchant selection, consumer convenience, and marketing efficiency.
- Mobility, Delivery, and Freight are reinforcing businesses. A consumer acquired for rides can use Eats, while restaurants and merchants benefit from the same demand and logistics infrastructure.
- The company is proving it can grow while expanding profitability: Q2 bookings grew 22% constant currency, adjusted EBITDA grew 33%, and the margin on bookings rose 40 basis points.
- Free cash flow is now substantial enough to fund product investment, acquisitions, autonomous-mobility partnerships, and share repurchases without requiring a balance-sheet transformation.
- Uber can become the distribution and dispatch layer for robotaxis and autonomous delivery, allowing it to benefit from autonomy without having to invent the entire vehicle stack. The Zoox partnership targets Las Vegas in 2026 and Los Angeles in 2027.
- The Zipline partnership brings autonomous drone delivery to Uber Eats, with a target of one million deliveries per day by 2029, adding long-term logistics optionality.

## Bear thesis

- Mobility remains exposed to worker-classification rules, minimum earnings requirements, insurance mandates, licensing, and local restrictions. These can raise prices or reduce driver supply.
- Autonomous vehicles could weaken Uber's marketplace economics if Waymo, Tesla, Zoox, or automakers own the customer relationship and capture more of the trip economics. Waymo and Uber have already ended their Phoenix partnership, illustrating that platform relationships are not guaranteed.
- Growth can be pressured by consumer weakness, lower discretionary travel, fuel/insurance costs, driver incentives, competition from Lyft and local platforms, and merchant dissatisfaction with Delivery take rates.
- Freight is cyclical and more exposed to industrial activity than Mobility or Delivery.
- GAAP earnings can be volatile because Uber holds strategic equity investments; investment revaluation gains should not be confused with recurring operating profit.
- International regulation, currency swings, privacy/cybersecurity issues, and legal proceedings can create volatility even when the marketplace metrics remain healthy.

## Catalysts

1. Continued 18%–22% constant-currency bookings growth with adjusted EBITDA growing faster than bookings.
2. Further expansion of Mobility and Delivery cross-use, MAPCs, trips per consumer, and advertising revenue.
3. Successful launches of Zoox and other autonomous partners on the Uber network without major safety or liability events.
4. Uber Eats drone and robot delivery scaling into a lower-variable-cost logistics layer.
5. Share repurchases supported by the company's growing free cash flow.

## What would change the view

**Upgrade:** bookings growth stays above 18%, adjusted EBITDA margin continues expanding, free cash flow remains above $10B annualized, and autonomous partnerships add rides without materially increasing capital intensity.  
**Downgrade:** repeated bookings deceleration below the mid-teens, margin contraction from incentives or regulation, weakening MAPCs/trips, materially higher legal or insurance costs, or autonomous platforms bypassing Uber's demand network.

## Bottom line

Uber is one of the better large-cap platform businesses in the watchlist: the network is mature, growth is still healthy, and cash generation is now a major strategic advantage. I classify it as **High conviction / accumulate on weakness**, with the caveat that the thesis depends on preserving marketplace economics as regulation and autonomy evolve. At roughly 17x trailing earnings and a strong free-cash-flow profile, UBER looks more attractive than many AI-adjacent growth names, provided the position is sized for regulatory and competitive volatility.
